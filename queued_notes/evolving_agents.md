# How to Build a Self-Improving Company with AI
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_JsIHUfUjc

*   The speaker prefaces the talk by mentioning it is conceptually high-level and draws inspiration from a recent talk by Diana and tweets from Jack Dorsey regarding the fundamental assumptions of how companies are built.
*   Historically, companies have been organized akin to Roman legions, which were designed to project power over vast distances (e.g., from Rome to Hadrian's Wall in Scotland).
*   This traditional structure relies on nested hierarchies and defined spans of control, where named individuals pass orders down and send information back up.
*   In this conventional model, human beings act as the primary conduit for information flowing up and down the organizational hierarchy.
*   Jack Dorsey's tweet highlighted the underlying assumption that this hierarchical organization is the optimal way to structure economic units of value.
*   The speaker argues that AI fundamentally breaks this assumption about organizational structure.
*   Initially, discussions around AI (about a year prior) focused heavily on productivity improvements, such as co-pilots making engineers 20% more productive or aiding in shipping software faster.
*   Viewing AI merely as a productivity tool—taking the old way of working and adding a more powerful engine—is considered a broken or limited way of thinking.
*   Instead of just boosting productivity, AI presents an opportunity to completely reimagine what a company is and how it functions.
*   AI models can potentially write more code than entire engineering teams, emphasizing the shift from simple productivity to comprehensive automation.
*   A core concept introduced is the extraction of domain knowledge from the company to define it as context for AI.
*   The ideal scenario is a self-improving loop: writing code, submitting a merge request, having an AI agent review it, merge it, and deploy it, allowing the system to handle similar queries successfully in the future without human intervention.
*   This self-improving loop is seen as far more valuable than a mere 20-30% productivity increase; it represents AI independently figuring out how to self-improve.
*   The goal is to identify areas within a company that can operate via these loops, reducing human involvement to a monitoring or supervisory capacity.
*   By throwing "tokens" (computational power/AI usage) at problems within these loops, a company can continuously get better on its own.
*   Examples of self-improving loops include analyzing product analytics to find friction points in a sales funnel, researching best practices, deploying AB tests, and automatically selecting and deploying the winning version.
*   Customer service queries can also be automated similarly, using AI agents acting as Chief Product Officer and Chief Technology Officer to evaluate suggestions, discard unaligned ones, and automatically write, deploy, and ship code for aligned suggestions overnight.
*   Viewing each part of a company as a self-improving recursive AI loop creates a stark contrast to the traditional, hierarchically organized Roman legion model.
*   The implications of this new model include a shift from measuring headcount to measuring token usage (computational expenditure).
*   Companies reaching demo day are currently generating about 5x more revenue per employee compared to 18 months ago, a trend expected to continue into later funding rounds (Series A and B).
*   Future constraints on companies will likely be based on token usage limits rather than headcount constraints.
*   While measuring individual token usage is currently a blunt and easily gameable metric, it directionally indicates who is maximizing the potential of AI within the organization.
*   The current phase is about extreme experimentation to discover the full capabilities of AI, avoiding premature formalization into strict leaderboards that invite gaming.
*   Middle management is predicted to become obsolete, as AI is better suited for the coordination problems middle management typically handles.
*   The speaker believes only two roles truly matter in this new paradigm: Individual Contributors (ICs) who act as builders/operators, and Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs).
*   DRIs must be named human beings, not committees or groups, to ensure accountability and get things done.
*   To build a self-improving company, the entire organization must be made "legible to AI," meaning absolutely everything must be recorded.
*   If an event, conversation, or decision is not recorded, it effectively did not happen from the AI's perspective.
*   Examples of recording include logging all partner emails into a database, and recording every Slack message, DM, and office hour session.
*   Because current AI context windows cannot process 100,000 hours of raw recordings, data must be diarized—aggregated, synthesized into important parts, and provided to the AI as breadcrumbs.
*   An example provided is the YC user manual, which was historically out of date. By using AI to process 2,000 hours of recently recorded office hours, a new, dramatically improved 150-page manual was generated over a weekend.
*   This new user manual can be updated monthly, automatically comparing new advice against existing content to either incorporate or discard it, creating a self-improving, living document.
*   When this living document is used as context for an AI agent, users can interact with a superintelligent system containing the combined wisdom of all partners.
*   Any artifact created within the company must be able to self-improve and be legible to AI; otherwise, it should be discarded.
*   Every function within the company can now generate on-demand internal software and dashboards, as current models (like Codex 55) are capable of one-shotting simple internal tools to a high level of quality.
*   Internal operations teams should rely on an underlying layer of intelligence rather than static software.
*   The generated software should be treated as ephemeral and disposable, while the underlying data and business context must be stored preciously (e.g., keeping emails in markdown format).
*   As AI models improve rapidly (e.g., every month or two), old internal software can simply be thrown away and regenerated using the original instructions and the newly improved model.
*   The true value lies in the business context and the skills/understanding inside people's heads, not the transient software built on top of it.
*   In this new model, the "company brain" consists of all aggregated data, emails, DMs, skills, and know-how.
*   The role of humans is to sit around the edges of this company brain, acting as the interface between the AI intelligence and the real world where models cannot yet go.
*   Humans are essential for novel situations, ethical considerations, high-stakes moments, and high-emotion scenarios (e.g., co-founder breakups or crucial sales conversations).
*   The speaker challenges the audience, noting that most are small enough to build their companies correctly from the start using this new AI-centric model, leaving no excuse to rely on outdated structures.
